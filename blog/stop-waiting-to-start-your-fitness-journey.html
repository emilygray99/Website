Stop Waiting to Start Your Fitness Journey (The Right Time Is Now)

Emily Gray , Masters Graduate Exercise Physiologist
20 July 20264 min readMindset

You already know what you want to do. You've known for a while.

You want to get stronger. Lose some fat. Feel better in your body. Have more energy. Stop waking up feeling like you're running behind on something you said you'd deal with "when things calm down."

But things haven't calmed down. They won't. And somewhere in the back of your mind, you know that too.

So let's talk about what's actually happening — because it's not a time problem.

The Reasons You're Waiting (And Why None of Them Are Real)

"I'll start Monday." "I just need to get through this busy period first." "I need to find the right program before I begin." "I'll start after the holidays." "I'll start when I have more energy." "I'm not ready yet."

I've heard every version of this. And I understand it — genuinely. Starting something new feels vulnerable. There's risk in beginning and not succeeding. Waiting feels safer because it keeps the possibility of success alive without requiring you to test it.

But here's what waiting actually does.

It doesn't prepare you. It doesn't make starting easier. It doesn't accumulate readiness that you can cash in at the right moment. It just takes time — time that passes regardless of whether you use it.

The women I've worked with who waited the longest to start weren't more ready when they finally did. They were more tired. More frustrated. More convinced that something must be wrong with them for not having figured this out yet.

There is nothing wrong with you. You've just been waiting for a starting line that doesn't exist.

Why Waiting Feels Like Preparation (But Isn't)

Your brain is very good at making inaction feel productive.

Planning your future workout schedule feels like progress. Reading about the best training split feels like preparation. Buying new gym gear feels like commitment. Waiting until after Easter, after the work project, after the kids go back to school feels like being strategic.

But none of it is the thing.

The thing is going. The thing is moving. The thing is doing one session, however imperfect, and then doing the next one.

As an Exercise Physiologist, I've never once had a client come to me and say they wished they'd waited longer to start. Not one. Every single woman I've worked with has, at some point, expressed some version of "I wish I'd done this sooner."

You don't need to be ready. You need to begin.

What "Starting" Actually Looks Like

Here's where I think the idea of starting gets overcomplicated.

Starting doesn't mean overhauling your entire life in a week. It doesn't mean a new meal plan, a new gym membership, a new wardrobe, and a 5am alarm on Monday morning.

Starting means doing the next smallest thing.

  • Book a gym session for tomorrow
  • Do a 20-minute walk today
  • Make one meal that prioritises protein
  • Put on your gym gear, even if you don't end up going anywhere

The goal of starting is not to be perfect. The goal is to generate evidence — evidence that you're someone who does this. Because that evidence is what confidence is built from. And you can't get it from the couch.

How to build a routine simple enough to start today is worth reading once you've finished here — it'll show you exactly what a realistic first week looks like.

The Cost of Waiting

This is the part I want you to sit with.

Every week you spend waiting is a week your future self doesn't have. The results you want — the stronger body, the more energy, the confidence in how you feel — they take time. Not years and years, but months of consistent effort. And those months start from whenever you begin.

If you start today, you're further along by Christmas. If you start in spring, you're further along by winter. The timeline moves with you. But it only moves if you start it.

I was overweight for years before I decided to change. I know what it's like to keep thinking the right moment is just around the corner. It isn't. The moment is now — messy, imperfect, inconvenient now. And now is enough.

You Don't Need a Perfect Start

You need a start.

Imperfect consistency will always beat perfect intention — that's not just a nice phrase, it's the mechanism behind every lasting transformation I've ever witnessed. The women who change their bodies and keep those results aren't the ones who started perfectly. They're the ones who started.

Here's your minimum for this week:

  • Two training sessions — whatever that looks like for you
  • Protein as your nutritional priority at most meals
  • A walk on the days you don't train

That's a start. That's enough. And next week, you build from there.

Stop waiting for Monday. Stop waiting for things to calm down. Stop waiting to feel ready.

Do the next thing. Then the next.

Now. There's no perfect moment, and waiting for one costs you weeks. Starting just means doing the next smallest thing, like booking a gym session for tomorrow or going for a 20-minute walk.

No. You need a start, not a perfect one. Imperfect consistency beats perfect intention, and planning or researching can feel like progress without being it.
