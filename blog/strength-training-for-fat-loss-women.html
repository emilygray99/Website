Why Strength Training is the Best Thing
You Can Do for Fat Loss (As a Woman)

Emily Gray , Masters Graduate Exercise Physiologist
12 June 20258 min readTraining

If you have been doing cardio consistently and not seeing the fat loss results you expected you aren't alone and you aren't doing it wrong. You're just missing the most powerful tool in the fat loss toolbox: resistance training.

This isn't an opinion. The evidence is clear and consistent. Here's exactly why strength training produces better fat loss outcomes for women than cardio alone and what to do about it.

Does cardio actually burn more fat than weights?

In a single session yes cardio typically burns more calories than a strength training session of equal duration. A 45-minute run burns more calories than a 45-minute weights session. This is why cardio feels productive for fat loss.

But this comparison misses what happens after the session ends. Strength training creates a phenomenon called excess post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC) your metabolic rate stays elevated for up to 38 hours after a resistance training session as your body repairs muscle tissue. Cardio produces minimal EPOC by comparison.

Elevated metabolic rate after resistance training (EPOC effect) cardio produces this for 3-14 hours at most
Source: Schuenke et al., European Journal of Applied Physiology (2002)

Why muscle mass is the key to sustainable fat loss

Your resting metabolic rate is the thing that drives long-term fat loss how many calories your body burns at rest. And the single biggest driver of resting metabolic rate is lean muscle mass.

Each kilogram of muscle tissue burns approximately 13 calories per day at rest compared to roughly 4.5 calories per kilogram of fat. This doesn't sound dramatic in isolation but across 10, 20 or 30 kilograms of muscle the difference compounds significantly.

When you lose weight through calorie restriction and cardio alone you typically lose a significant amount of muscle alongside fat. A 2021 meta-analysis published in Obesity Reviews found that calorie restriction without resistance training resulted in roughly 25–35% of total weight loss coming from lean mass. That means for every 10kg lost on a cardio-only approach, 2.5–3.5kg of that's muscle directly reducing your metabolic rate and setting you up for weight regain.

The cycle this creates: Lose weight (including muscle) → metabolic rate drops → maintain weight briefly → gain it back faster than before. Sound familiar? This is the physiological mechanism behind yo-yo dieting and strength training is the main tool that breaks it.

What does the research actually say?

A comprehensive 2012 review in Current Sports Medicine Reports compared cardio-only resistance-only and combined training approaches for fat loss. Key findings:

  • Cardio-only produced the greatest immediate weight loss but the lowest muscle retention
  • Resistance-only produced less total weight loss but significantly better body composition outcomes (more fat lost less muscle lost)
  • Combined training (both) produced the best overall outcomes greatest fat loss best muscle retention best long-term metabolic rate

For women specifically a 2019 study in the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research found that women who combined resistance training with a moderate calorie deficit lost 32% more fat mass compared to those who only dieted despite consuming the same number of calories.

More fat lost by women who added resistance training to their calorie deficit vs diet alone same calories consumed

Will I get bulky if I lift weights?

This is the most common concern I hear and it is one of the most persistent myths in women's fitness. The short answer is no and the physiology explains why.

Women have approximately 10–20 times less testosterone than men. Testosterone is the primary anabolic hormone responsible for large muscle growth. Without it in significant quantities the kind of muscle bulk most women fear is physiologically very difficult to achieve it typically requires years of deliberate mass-building training significant calorie surplus and in many cases pharmaceutical assistance.

What progressive strength training produces in women in a calorie deficit is a leaner more defined physique less body fat more muscle tone better posture and stronger joints. The aesthetic most women describe as their goal is actually the result of strength training not the thing to avoid from it.

How do you actually get started with strength training?

For women new to resistance training a 3-day per week full-body program built around compound movements is the most evidence-supported starting point:

  • Squat pattern goblet squats leg press or barbell back squat
  • Hinge pattern , Romanian deadlifts or conventional deadlifts
  • Push pattern dumbbell press chest press machine or push-ups
  • Pull pattern lat pulldown seated row or dumbbell row
  • Carry or core farmer's carry plank variations

3 sets of 8–12 reps per exercise with progressive overload (gradually increasing weight) week to week. This structure is the foundation of every program I write for clients and it produces results consistently.

The most important variable: consistency over time. Three sessions per week for 12 weeks produces significantly greater results than six sessions per week for three weeks. The women who see the best fat loss outcomes are the ones who train reliably not heroically.

The bottom line

Cardio isn't bad. It's good for cardiovascular health mood and contributes to overall calorie burn. But for sustainable fat loss the kind where you lose the weight and keep it off strength training is the most important training tool you have.

It preserves muscle. It keeps your metabolism elevated. It produces better body composition outcomes. And it builds the physical capacity and confidence that makes training something you actually want to maintain long term.

Common questions

Strength training for women , answered

Is strength training or cardio better for fat loss in women?+

Strength training produces superior long-term fat loss results. While cardio burns more calories during the session strength training increases resting metabolic rate by building and preserving muscle resulting in more calories burned 24/7. A combination is optimal but if prioritising one resistance training has stronger evidence for sustainable fat loss in women.

Will lifting weights make women bulky?+

No. Women have approximately 10–20 times less testosterone than men making significant muscle bulk physiologically very difficult to achieve without deliberate years-long effort. Progressive strength training in a calorie deficit produces a leaner more defined physique not bulk.

How many days per week should women strength train for fat loss?+

Research supports 3–4 resistance training sessions per week for women seeking fat loss. This frequency provides adequate stimulus for muscle retention and metabolic adaptation while allowing sufficient recovery time between sessions.

Can beginners do strength training for weight loss?+

Yes beginners often see the fastest results because the body is highly responsive to a new training stimulus. Starting with compound movements 3 days per week produces significant improvements in strength and body composition within 8–12 weeks.
