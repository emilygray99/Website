How Much Protein Do Women
Actually Need for Weight Loss?

Emily Gray , Masters Graduate Exercise Physiologist
19 June 20256 min readNutrition

Protein is the single most important nutrition variable for women trying to lose weight sustainably. More important than cutting carbs. More important than avoiding fat. More important than meal timing. And yet the vast majority of women I work with are eating roughly half the protein they need to see the results they want.

Here's exactly how much you need why it matters so much and the simplest ways to actually hit your target without weighing every meal.

How much protein do women need per day?

The Australian Recommended Daily Intake (RDI) for protein is 0.75–0.8g per kg of bodyweight. This figure is designed to prevent deficiency in sedentary adults not to support fat loss preserve muscle during a calorie deficit or optimise body composition.

For women actively trying to lose weight the evidence points to a substantially higher target. A 2020 position paper from the International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) recommends 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day for individuals in a calorie deficit aiming to preserve lean mass.

1.6–2.2g
Protein per kg of bodyweight per day the evidence-based target for women losing weight
Source: Stokes et al., Nutrients (2018); ISSN Position Stand (2017)

In practical terms for a 70kg woman this translates to approximately 112–154g of protein per day. For a 60kg woman it is approximately 96–132g. Compare this to the Australian average protein intake for women which sits around 60–70g per day and the gap becomes clear.

Why does protein matter so much for weight loss?

It has a high thermic effect

Your body burns approximately 20–30% of protein calories during digestion and metabolism compared to 5–10% for carbohydrates and 0–3% for fat. This means eating 100 calories of protein effectively costs 20–30 calories to process. At scale this adds up.

It preserves muscle during a calorie deficit

When you eat at a calorie deficit your body looks for energy wherever it can find it including from muscle tissue. Adequate protein intake signals to your body to preserve that muscle. Maintaining muscle mass during fat loss is critical because muscle is metabolically active: it raises your resting metabolic rate meaning your body burns more calories at rest.

It keeps you fuller for longer

Protein stimulates the release of satiety hormones including GLP-1 and PYY while suppressing ghrelin (the hunger hormone). A high-protein meal keeps most people satisfied for significantly longer than the same calorie load from carbohydrates or fat making a calorie deficit far easier to maintain.

How do you actually hit your protein target?

Most women find it easiest to work backwards from their target and build each meal around a protein source rather than trying to add protein into an existing meal structure.

A practical framework for a 70kg woman targeting 130g of protein per day:

  • Breakfast: 2 eggs + Greek yoghurt ≈ 32g
  • Lunch: Chicken or tuna salad ≈ 45g
  • Dinner: Salmon or beef + legumes ≈ 40g
  • Snack: Cottage cheese or protein shake ≈ 20g

That totals approximately 137g without tracking every gram or eating anything unusual.

The key insight: You don't need to eat chicken and broccoli every meal. You need to identify your protein sources portion them consistently and build the rest of the meal around them. The variety takes care of itself.

The bottom line

The Australian RDI for protein wasn't designed for women in a calorie deficit trying to lose fat and preserve muscle. The target I use with clients is 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day. Most women are eating less than half this amount and wondering why fat loss feels so hard.

Fix your protein first. Everything else becomes easier when you do.

Common questions

Protein for women , answered

How much protein should a woman eat per day to lose weight?+

Research supports 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day for women in a calorie deficit. For a 70kg woman that's 112–154g of protein per day significantly higher than the Australian RDI of 0.75g/kg which is designed for general health maintenance not fat loss.

What are the best high protein foods for women?+

Top protein sources include chicken breast (~31g per 100g) canned tuna (~25g per 100g), Greek yoghurt (~10g per 100g) eggs (~6g each) cottage cheese (~12g per 100g) and lentils (~9g per 100g cooked). Protein powder can supplement but shouldn't replace whole food protein sources.

Does eating more protein help with weight loss?+

Yes. Protein has a higher thermic effect than carbohydrates or fat your body burns approximately 20–30% of protein calories during digestion. It also increases satiety hormones and suppresses ghrelin making it easier to stay in a calorie deficit without feeling constantly hungry.

Can you eat too much protein as a woman?+

For most healthy women intakes up to 2.2g/kg bodyweight are well-tolerated and safe. Current evidence doesn't support concerns about high protein intake causing kidney damage in healthy individuals. If you have a pre-existing kidney condition consult your GP before significantly increasing protein intake.
